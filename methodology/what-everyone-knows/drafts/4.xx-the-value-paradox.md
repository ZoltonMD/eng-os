# The Value Paradox: Engineering Integrity vs. Budget Cynicism [DRAFT]

I once had a CTO tell me that the engineering department is "just a cost". The logic was that engineers don't generate revenue like sales or account management do. Back then, I temporary lost my motivation for couple days. *"Can an engineer value the CTO who does not value his engineers?"* I thought.
I can't agree with his point of view. And it was this disagreement that led to the chapter you are currently reading.

## The "Cost Center" Fallacy

The logic inside this kind of managers is pretty simple: developers has written a code, SRE prepared infrastructure, now all you need is just deploy the code into prepared infrastructure and it will make money itself. 
But this is the line of reasoning of someone who isn't technically inclined and doesn't understand how the system actually works.
It is our job to convey the true state of affairs to the managers.

If we put ourselves in their shoes, we can understand their point of view. All they see is a "Black Box". On one side they see money flowing into this "black box", on other side they see new features emerging from it.

But they do not see the maintenance, the entropy, or the risk mitigation. When you automate another "toil" process, only your team sees that you saved another 200 man-hours per year (and as we know $Time=Money$). But from a business point of view—they (company) just spent another money for the system maintenance. It's especially related to SRE, if everything is working perfectly, it looks like you are doing nothing. This is **SRE Paradox**.  

The only point when an engineer's work becomes visible is the time when something doesn't work. This creates very dangerous connection between *Engineers* and *losing of money* inside top-level managers' minds.  

So, are engineers really just a cost? Honestly, I don't believe in that because this claim is beyond common sense and logic. Engineers build the engine that drives the business. Without that engine, even a Ferrari is just a very expensive, stationary sofa. It might look good in the showroom, but it’s not going anywhere.  

> *Coders, SREs, architects, ITSecs are partners who understand how technology enables business, and how bits become a real money.* 

## The Math of Invisible Value
To confirm my theory, let's run a simple mental health check: *"What is the revenue of your top-tier sales team when system uptime is zero?"*. Exactly. It’s zero. Revenue is not a constant; it is a function of uptime $(U)$ and performance $(P)$.

$$Revenue=∫(Sales⋅Performance)dt⋅Uptime$$

If $Uptime=0$, then $Revenue=0$.

Engineering value is often measured in what *didn't* happen (outages, data leaks, slow-downs). Business struggles to value "The Silence", but as SREs, **Silence is our greatest product**.  
We are doing everything in our power to keep the system in a state where, from the perspective of users and businesses, it appears as though nothing is happening—even though, in reality, that is not always the case. All these Replication Factors, Disaster Recovery clusters, graceful switching users between nodes during a maintenance, etc. exist only for one purpose—to enable business to make more money by ensuring uninterrupted uptime.  

I understand, It's very difficult to measure how well something isn't happening. You can't use for these measurements the same gauge you use for sales. When you produce tires, you can just count how many tires you have produced in current month/quarter/year. But if you start counting how many incidents you fixed—I have bad news for you—you are in a dead end.  
This is why the most of KPI systems doesn't work in SRE—and many other engineering professions whose main product is *The Silence*—world. But that doesn’t mean this effort isn’t worth making.
